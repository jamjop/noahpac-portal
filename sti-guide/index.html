HIV prevention

PrEP / PEP quick workflow

CDC · reviewed Oct 2026

A focused starting and follow-up checklist. Use current CDC guidance or the National Clinician Consultation Center for regimen selection in pregnancy, pediatrics, renal disease, resistance, or other complex situations.

Possible exposure ≤72 hours: PEP
  1. Start immediately. Treat as a medical emergency—ideally within 24 hours and no later than 72 hours. Do not delay the first dose while awaiting labs or source testing.
  2. Confirm the exposure. Assess mucous-membrane/nonintact-skin or percutaneous contact with potentially infectious fluid and source HIV status when available.
  3. Baseline: blood HIV Ag/Ab; HIV NAT when indicated by recent injectable cabotegravir; pregnancy test when relevant; creatinine, AST/ALT; HBV/HCV; and site-specific GC/CT plus syphilis testing.
  4. Use a complete 28-day, three-drug regimen from current CDC guidance. Adjust for age, pregnancy, renal function, drug interactions, and resistance concerns; obtain expert help without delaying initiation.
  5. Follow up: contact within 24 hours, then HIV Ag/Ab plus NAT at 4–6 and 12 weeks after exposure. Transition directly to PrEP after PEP when ongoing exposure is anticipated and HIV is excluded.

Exposure >72 hours: PEP is generally not recommended; test for HIV, address prevention needs, and seek expert input when circumstances are exceptional.

Ongoing prevention: PrEP
  1. Offer broadly. Inform all sexually active adults and adolescents; prescribe to anyone who asks, including those who do not report risk factors.
  2. Exclude HIV before starting. Use a blood Ag/Ab test and HIV-1 RNA when indicated by recent PrEP/PEP or possible acute infection. Avoid oral rapid tests. Defer PrEP when results are discordant or acute HIV is unresolved.
  3. Baseline: site-specific GC/CT and syphilis testing. For oral tenofovir, calculate Cockcroft–Gault eCrCl and screen for HBV; add lipids for F/TAF.
  4. Match the option: F/TDF requires eCrCl >60 and covers sexual or injection risk; F/TAF requires eCrCl ≥30 but is not indicated for receptive vaginal sex; injectable CAB does not require kidney testing and follows initial, one-month, then every-two-month dosing.
  5. Follow up: oral PrEP HIV Ag/Ab plus RNA and no more than a 90-day supply every 3 months; renal and STI monitoring at CDC-defined intervals. CAB requires HIV testing at month 1 and every 2 months with injections.

2-1-1 oral PrEP is off-label, not FDA-approved, and not recommended by CDC. Active HBV is not a contraindication to oral PrEP, but stopping tenofovir can cause hepatitis flares and requires follow-up.
